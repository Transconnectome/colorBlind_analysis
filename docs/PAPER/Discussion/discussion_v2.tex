\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

In this study, we tested whether \emph{individual hV4 colour geometry} is sufficiently structured to ground a person-specific corrective filter in colour vision deficiency.
We found that two CVD adults retained 8-class hue discrimination at hV4 but selectively lost continuous-hue interpolation, and that the residual distortion was captured by a 2-component angular-dilation model whose pre-image was exact for all eight hues in both participants (sub-08 mean $|\delta| = 22.2^\circ$; sub-09 mean $|\delta| = 10.2^\circ$).
These results suggest that the cortical site at which CVD colour information is geometrically distorted is also the site at which it can be inverted into a stimulus-space correction.

The dissociation between LOCO interpolation capacity and SRM $\Delta$RDM geometric distance --- $100\%$ vs.\ $33\%$ concordance with same-day JND thresholds --- identifies which cortical quantity is the operative corrective target.
Representational geometry (RDM) reflects the metric structure of neural colour space; LOCO interpolation reflects whether that structure supports functional prediction of withheld hues.
That only LOCO predicts behavioural colour confusions is consistent with \citeA{brouwer2009}, who showed that hV4 --- but not early visual areas --- supports interpolation to held-out hues in typical observers.
The present results extend this dissociation to these two CVD cases: the same interpolation failure that predicts JND confusion is what the correction filter must address.
Geometric distance metrics alone are insufficient to identify it.

The S-cone axis component ($\beta_s$) of the fitted distortion field is structurally consistent with prior evidence for cortical colour compensation in anomalous trichromats.
\citeA{emery2021} reported that CVD perceptual errors concentrate near S-cone confusion loci in behavioural hue-scaling tasks, and \citeA{tregillus2021} showed that V2/V3 BOLD responses in anomalous trichromats include a cortical gain component ($g$) that partially compensates the retinal cone shift.
These individually structured hV4 residuals may reflect cone-class-specific differences in V2/V3 compensation gain that survive into hV4, where the incompletely cancelled L--M signal manifests as a person-specific distortion of the continuous hue code.
This suggests that the present 2-component model extends earlier population-mean findings to the individual hV4 level: the residual distortion that survives V2/V3 compensation reaches hV4 as an individually structured pattern.
The two CVD participants in fact present qualitatively distinct correction signatures rather than mirror-image realisations of a single mechanism.
Sub-08 (deutan) exhibits a wide-amplitude L/M-rotation with large $\hat\beta_s = 38^\circ$ and a confusion-axis term $\hat\beta_c = -14^\circ$; the R+C diagnostic decomposition recovers a near-zero retinal shift ($\Delta\lambda \approx 2.5$~nm) coupled with a large cortical overshoot ($g = -2.25$), pointing to a cortical-dominant CVD etiology.
Sub-09 (protan), by contrast, exhibits a far smaller monotonic rotation along the protan confusion axis (small $\hat\beta_s = 6^\circ$, $\hat\beta_c = -22^\circ$); the R+C diagnostic recovers a large retinal shift ($\Delta\lambda \approx 19.5$~nm, within the published protan cone-shift range \cite{tregillus2021}) and a near-physiological cortical compensation ($g = -1.10$), pointing to a retinal-dominant etiology.
This cortical-vs-retinal etiological dissociation is what a population-average retinal model, applied uniformly to all members of a given CVD subtype, fails to capture: in sub-09 a retinal cone-shift correction matches the diagnosed mechanism, whereas in sub-08 the residual distortion is generated downstream of the cone shift and cannot be neutralised by a spectral filter alone.

All distortion models --- 1-DOF Machado, 2-DOF R+C, and 2-component --- identify the same two participants as requiring correction, but their prescribed correction vectors diverge in direction (Appendix~\ref{app:altmodels}).
This detection--correction dissociation constitutes the study's primary falsifiable prediction.
In a 2AFC experiment comparing the 2-component and retinal-family filter conditions for each participant's most LOCO-vulnerable hue pairs, only the cortically-grounded 2-component filter should significantly reduce JND.
If the retinal-family filter matches or exceeds the 2-component filter in reducing discrimination thresholds, the cortical distortion account would require revision.
This preregistered comparison directly tests whether cortical or retinal distortion is the corrective target for CVD colour correction.

An alternative reading is that the hV4 LOCO impairment merely reflects reduced V1--V3 input variance rather than cortex-level distortion --- that the bottleneck is upstream signal quality and hV4 inherits its deficit passively.
The preserved LORO accuracy at hV4 argues against this account: classification and interpolation read out from the same upstream cortical input, so a generic input-variance reduction should degrade both.
The observed dissociation instead localises the distortion to the continuous-hue manifold readout, consistent with hV4 as the cortical site of integration over cone-opponent signals~\cite{brouwer2009, bannert2018}.

Four considerations bound the proof-of-concept scope of these findings.
First, the CVD sample is $N = 2$; single-case statistics \cite{crawford1998} permit individual-level inference but population-level claims about deutan or protan subtypes require replication with larger samples.
Second, the confusion-axis component ($\beta_c$) is statistically significant for Sub-08 only.
For Sub-09, the bootstrap confidence interval for $\beta_c$ includes zero, and the 2-component model reduces to an effectively 1-component fit.
The \emph{2-component} label should not be read as confirming a two-mechanism account for both participants; power-adequate replication or hierarchical-Bayes pooling across deutan and protan subtypes is required to determine whether the second component is genuinely absent in protan.
Third, the voxel-prediction LOCO loss family under which the 2-component model is fit yields a $100\%$ false-positive rate when applied to HC participants under label permutation (HC FPR $= 7/7$; Supplementary~\ref{app:hc_specificity}).
We therefore report per-subject p-values as descriptive of representational-geometry fit, not as specificity statements that distinguish CVD from HC.
The effective HC pool ($n = 6$ at hV4 after excluding sub-07 for low voxel count) is too small to anchor a formal specificity test, and the per-subject $\Delta\rho$ statistic is dominated by a baseline correlation confound (HC distribution Pearson $r = -0.894$ between baseline $\rho$ and $\Delta\rho$) that precludes within-cohort selectivity claims.
Case-level confidence therefore rests on convergence across multiple anchors --- cross-criterion fit significance, physiological fit magnitude relative to HC LOO refits, and S-cone axis grounding --- rather than on a single permutation-based test.
Fourth, external cross-cohort hue-scaling and Phase-3 2AFC discrimination data will arbitrate cortical-level specificity beyond the within-cohort permutation null.

This pipeline constitutes a proof-of-concept that \emph{individual hV4 colour geometry}, measured via fMRI LOCO decoding and inverted through an angular-dilation model, can ground a colour correction that is architecturally distinct from population-average retinal approaches.
Where Machado-type correction applies a fixed spectral shift to all individuals of a given CVD subtype, the present approach encodes each individual's hV4 distortion field and inverts it exactly --- without assuming population homogeneity.
Phase~3 will test whether this cortical-level correction reduces discrimination thresholds where retinal-level correction does not.
