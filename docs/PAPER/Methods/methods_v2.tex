\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

The analysis follows three stages.
First, the fMRI neural signature of each CVD participant's hue-processing deficit was characterized by two measures, both serving as fitting criteria for the distortion model. Leave-one-color-out (LOCO) decoding at hV4 yielded a hue vulnerability profile $\mathbf{v} \in [0,1]^8$ (see the \nameref{sec:methods:loco} section). The between-group difference of representational dissimilarity matrices (RDMs), denoted $\Delta$RDM, quantified the per-pair deviation of CVD geometry from the HC mean (see the \nameref{sec:methods:rdm} section).
Second, a two-component cortical distortion model fits these neural observations by simulating the joint effect of a retinal cone shift and a cortical orientation gain on HC-trained neural representations (see the \nameref{sec:methods:twocomp} section).
Third, the fitted distortion is analytically inverted to yield a stimulus-space pre-image filter that maps the distorted hue coordinates back towards the HC percept (see the \nameref{sec:methods:filter} section).

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Participants}
\label{sec:methods:participants}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Thirteen volunteers were recruited under Institutional Review Board approval (\#2510/002-023) and provided written informed consent.
Color vision was screened using the 14-plate Ishihara pseudoisochromatic test \cite{ishihara1917}.
Participants identifying $\geq 9$ plates correctly were classified as healthy controls (HC; $n = 7$, 3 female, age $22.7 \pm 2.5$ years).
Two male participants scoring below this threshold were classified as CVD: sub-08 (deuteranomalous, 5/14 plates corrected) and sub-09 (protanomalous, 7/14 plates corrected).
Four additional participants were excluded for failing to complete the full scanning protocol.
All CVD results are interpreted as single-case demonstrations using the \citeA{crawford1998} modified $t$-test; no population-level inference is made.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Stimuli and task}
\label{sec:methods:stimuli}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Eight isoluminant chromatic stimuli were drawn from CIE $L^{*}a^{*}b^{*}$ space at equal angular spacing ($0^\circ$--$315^\circ$, $45^\circ$ steps; $L^{*} = 75$, chroma $= 40$) \cite{cie1986}, spanning red, orange, yellow, green, cyan, blue, purple, and magenta (Figure~\ref{fig:paradigm}A).
A gray filler ($a^{*} = b^{*} = 0$) served as a null condition.

Stimuli were presented in a Rapid Serial Visual Presentation (RSVP) design to sustain attention and fixation.
A circular patch (1.5 s per stimulus) was shown in pseudo-random order optimized with Neurodesign \cite{durnez2018}.
Participants pressed a button upon detecting two consecutive 'K's within a rapid serial letter stream presented above the grating patch.
Each run contained eight repetitions of all eight chromatic stimuli and the gray filler; inter-stimulus intervals were drawn from $\{3, 4.5, 6\}$ s.
Each session comprised six runs ($\approx 7$ min each).
The experiment was coded in PsychoPy 2022.2.5 \cite{peirce2019}.

CVD participants additionally completed a second scanning session for filter evaluation (see the \nameref{sec:methods:behavioral} section).

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{MRI acquisition and preprocessing}
\label{sec:methods:mri}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Data were acquired on a Siemens 3T MAGNETOM Cima.X scanner with BioMatrix 3T coils.
To constrain coverage to posterior occipital cortex we used 24 oblique slices perpendicular to the calcarine sulcus \cite{brouwer2009} (TR 1.5 s, TE 30 ms, FA $75^\circ$, $2 \times 2 \times 2$ mm, $96 \times 80$ matrix).

Data were converted to BIDS format and defaced with ezBIDS \cite{levitas2024}.
Functional images were registered to the T1w image via mutual-information coregistration (FreeSurfer \texttt{mri\_coreg}; \citeNP{maes1997, wells1996}).
T1w-to-MNI normalization used 12-DOF affine (FSL FLIRT; \citeNP{jenkinson2002}) followed by nonlinear warping (FNIRT; \citeNP{andersson2007}), yielding 2 mm isotropic MNI-space BOLD data.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{ROI definition and response estimation}
\label{sec:methods:roi}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Bilateral V1, V2, V3, and hV4 were defined from the Wang probabilistic atlas \cite{wang2015} at $> 50\%$ probability, intersected with each participant's BOLD brain mask (voxel counts: V1 $655 \pm 214$; V2 $451 \pm 145$; V3 $103 \pm 29$; hV4 $63 \pm 22$; mean $\pm$ SD).

Single-trial response amplitudes were estimated with a two-stage GLM \cite{dale1999, brouwer2009, brouwer2013}.
First, an ROI-level hemodynamic response function (HRF) was extracted from a finite-impulse-response (FIR) model (8-TR basis set).
Voxels in the top 50\% of variance explained by the mean ROI HRF were retained (V1 $328 \pm 107$; V2 $226 \pm 73$; V3 $52 \pm 15$; hV4 $32 \pm 11$ voxels).
Second, the estimated HRF was convolved with per-color design matrices and fitted by ridge regression to yield one amplitude per (voxel, color, run), organized as a $6 \times 8 \times V$ array per subject.

Voxel amplitudes were aligned across runs using within-subject Procrustes rotation \cite{gower1975}: runs 2--6 were mapped to run 1 via the orthogonal transformation minimizing $\|X_\text{ref} - XQ\|_F$ (scaling prohibited).

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Shared Response Model}
\label{sec:methods:srm}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Cross-subject alignment and dimensionality reduction were performed with the Shared Response Model (SRM; \citeNP{chen2015}).
SRM estimates per-participant orthonormal mappings $W_i \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times k}$ ($V$ voxels, $k$ latent dimensions) and a group-shared response matrix $S \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times 8}$:

\begin{equation}
  \min_{W_i,\,S}\;\sum_{i=1}^N \|X_i - W_i S\|_F^2 \quad\text{s.t.}\quad W_i^\top W_i = I_k
\end{equation}

SRM was trained exclusively on the seven HC participants to avoid circular inference in CVD comparisons.
Each CVD participant was subsequently projected into the fixed HC-derived space by solving $W_\text{CVD} = UV^\top$ where $U\Sigma V^\top = \text{SVD}(X_\text{CVD}\cdot S^\dagger)$.
Reduced dimensions were chosen by mean-rank aggregation across seven-fold leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) cross-validation, using three SRM-quality metrics (RDM split-half reliability, cross-subject RDM correlation, and reconstruction error): V1 $k = 4$; V2 $k = 4$; V3 $k = 3$; hV4 $k = 3$.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Forward encoding model}
\label{sec:methods:encoding}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Both discrimination (LORO) and interpolation (LOCO) decoding used the same forward encoding model \cite{brouwer2009}.
The model represents each stimulus as a $F = 6$-dimensional channel vector $\mathbf{c}(\theta) = [c_1(\theta), \ldots, c_F(\theta)]^\top$, where $c_k(\theta) = \max\!\bigl(0,\,\cos(\theta - \theta_k)\bigr)^2$ is a half-wave rectified squared cosine tuned to preferred hue $\theta_k$ (preferred hues spaced equally at $60^\circ$; \citeNP{brouwer2009}).
A weight matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{F \times V}$ is estimated by ridge regression with the regularization parameter $\alpha$ selected by generalized cross-validation (GCV; \citeNP{golub1979}; see Supplementary~\ref{app:gcv} for implementation details) to predict voxel amplitudes from channel activations: $\hat{X} = CW^\top$.
Decoding inverts the encoding direction: for a held-out voxel pattern $\mathbf{x}$, channel activations are reconstructed as $\hat{\mathbf{c}} = W\mathbf{x}$, and the candidate hue whose basis vector has the highest Pearson correlation with $\hat{\mathbf{c}}$ is returned as the decoded color (Figure~\ref{fig:forward}).
Alternative decoders evaluated during model selection are described in Appendix~\ref{app:altmodels}.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Evaluating color discrimination with cross-run decoding}
\label{sec:methods:loro}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Categorical discrimination was assessed with leave-one-run-out (LORO) cross-validation in SRM-aligned space using the forward encoding model (see the \nameref{sec:methods:encoding} section).
For each of the six runs, $W$ was estimated on the remaining five runs using mean-subtracted SRM projections, and each held-out run's 8 color patterns were decoded by nearest-neighbour matching.
Primary discrimination performance was the 8-class exact accuracy (chance $= 1/8 = 0.125$), averaged across six folds.

Group-level HC--CVD comparison used a Mann--Whitney $U$ test on cross-subject generalization scores: the encoding model trained on each HC participant's SRM-aligned data was applied to every other participant's aligned patterns, yielding 21 unique HC-to-HC and 14 HC-to-CVD pairwise generalization scores.
Within-ROI single-case tests used the \citeA{crawford1998} modified $t$-test (one-tailed, lower).

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Evaluating color interpolation with cross-color decoding}
\label{sec:methods:loco}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Continuous hue interpolation was assessed with leave-one-color-out (LOCO) cross-validation using the same forward encoding model (see the \nameref{sec:methods:encoding} section).
Each of the 8 hues was held out in turn; $W$ was estimated on the remaining 7 hues using all 6 runs (pooled $42$-sample design matrix), and the held-out hue was predicted by nearest-neighbour matching in channel-output space.

\paragraph{Adjacent accuracy}
The primary cross-color decoding metric was adjacent accuracy: the proportion of predictions that fell within $\pm 1$ hue step ($45^\circ$) of the target hue, averaged across the 6 runs of each test color (range $0$--$1$; chance level under a uniform random predictor $= 3/8 = 0.375$; exact-accuracy chance $= 1/8 = 0.125$).
For HC above-chance testing and for pairwise HC--CVD comparisons at hV4, $p$-values were obtained from 1,000 random color-label permutations and from the \citeA{crawford1998} modified $t$-test (one-tailed, lower), respectively.

\paragraph{Vulnerability profile}
The 8-dimensional vector of per-hue adjacent-accuracy values, $\mathbf{v} \in [0,1]^8$, constitutes each participant's \emph{hue vulnerability profile}.
For group-level model fit, we report Spearman $\rho$ between the distortion-model-predicted and observed $\mathbf{v}$ (see the \nameref{sec:methods:twocomp} section).
Per-hue group comparisons (Figure~\ref{fig:loco}C) used the \citeA{crawford1998} modified $t$-test (one-tailed, uncorrected).
Effect sizes are reported as Hedges' $d$ \cite{hedges1985} with the Crawford--Howell correction for small-$n$ comparisons.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Between-group representational dissimilarity}
\label{sec:methods:rdm}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

To characterize the representational geometry of LOCO impairment in SRM-aligned space, we computed two measures.
Mean pairwise disparity captures whether CVD geometry is globally more dispersed than HC.
The between-group RDM difference, $\Delta$RDM, captures which color pairs drive the deviation and tests whether a cortical distortion model accounts for the pattern.

\paragraph{Pairwise disparity}
For each participant and ROI, the RDM was the $8 \times 8$ matrix of pairwise correlation distances ($1 - \text{Pearson}\,r$).
Mean pairwise disparity was the average of the 28 upper-triangle elements.
To provide an unbiased HC reference for the held-out HC participant, the HC disparity distribution was estimated by a leave-one-subject-out (LOO) procedure.
Under the directional prediction that CVD geometry is more dispersed than HC, individual CVD disparity was compared to the HC LOO distribution by the \citeA{crawford1998} modified $t$-test (one-tailed, upper).
Significance was assessed at $\alpha = 0.05$.

\paragraph{Geometric deviation from HC and model correspondence}
The per-pair deviation of each CVD participant's RDM from the HC mean was $\Delta\text{RDM} = \text{RDM}_\text{CVD} - \overline{\text{RDM}}_\text{HC}$, where $\overline{\text{RDM}}_\text{HC}$ is the grand mean RDM over the seven HC participants.
Correspondence between the observed $\Delta\text{RDM}$ and the prediction of the two-component distortion model (see \nameref{sec:methods:twocomp}) was reported descriptively as the Spearman $\rho$ between the upper-triangle elements of the two matrices.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Two-component distortion model}
\label{sec:methods:twocomp}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CVD hue distortion was modelled as a two-component angular shift in Stockman opponent-color space:

\begin{equation}
  \delta\theta(h;\,\beta_s,\beta_c) = \beta_s \cos(h - 90^\circ) + \beta_c \cos(h - \theta_{\rm conf})
  \label{eq:2comp}
\end{equation}

where $h$ is the opponent-hue angle of the stimulus in CIELab space and $\theta_{\rm conf}$ is the CVD-subtype confusion axis ($16^\circ$ protan; $150^\circ$ deutan), derived from the Brettel--Vi\'enot--Mollon dichromatic simulation \cite{brettel1997} applied to Stockman \& Sharpe LMS cone fundamentals \cite{stockman2000}. $\beta_s$ parameterizes distortion along the S-cone axis ($h = 90^\circ$), motivated by cortical-RDM evidence that CVD errors concentrate at S-cone loci \cite{emery2021}. $\beta_c$ parameterizes rotation along the confusion axis, with both signs unconstrained as free parameters of the fit.
We compared this model with retinal-shift-based alternatives (Machado, R+C retinal family); see Appendix~\ref{app:altmodels}.

Per-subject parameters $(\beta_s, \beta_c)$ were recovered by minimizing a four-term, neural-primary loss derived from the V4 LOCO forward model:

\begin{equation}
  L_\text{fit}(\beta_s, \beta_c) = 1.0\,L_\text{vuln} + 0.5\,L_\text{rank} + 0.2\,L_\text{rdm} + 0.1\,L_\text{smooth}
  \label{eq:lfit}
\end{equation}

with each term normalized to $[0,1]$ before weighting.
Let $\mathbf{v}_\text{sim}(\beta_s, \beta_c) = f_\text{forward}\!\bigl(C(\theta + \delta\theta(\,\cdot\,;\beta_s,\beta_c));\,W\bigr) \in [0,1]^8$ be the simulated per-color V4 LOCO vulnerability ($1 - \rho$) obtained by re-applying the HC-trained ridge encoding weights $W$ (Section~\ref{sec:methods:encoding}) to the design matrix $C$ shifted by the candidate distortion, and let $\mathbf{v}_\text{obs} \in [0,1]^8$ be the observed CVD per-color vulnerability.

\paragraph{Loss-term roles.}
$L_\text{vuln} = \tfrac{1}{8 \cdot 4}\,\|\mathbf{v}_\text{sim} - \mathbf{v}_\text{obs}\|^2$ (per-color MSE, normalizer $4.0$ for $[0,1]$ amplitude range) is the primary fit term: it forces the simulated vulnerability profile to match the per-color LOCO breakdown that the CVD encoder exhibits, which is the operative neural quantity for the corrective filter (see the \nameref{sec:discussion} section for the LOCO--behavior concordance evidence underlying this choice).
$L_\text{rank} = (1 - \rho_\text{Spearman}(\mathbf{v}_\text{sim}, \mathbf{v}_\text{obs}))/2$ is an ordering term that breaks ties when the MSE landscape is locally flat by penalizing rank-reordering of vulnerable colors; it is downweighted to $0.5$ because amplitude (MSE) is the operative signal.
$L_\text{rdm} = (1 - \cos(\Delta\text{RDM}_\text{sim}, \Delta\text{RDM}_\text{obs}))/2$ is a geometric convergence check on the 28-pair upper-triangle of the CVD-minus-HC representational dissimilarity-matrix difference at hV4 ($\Delta\text{RDM} = \text{RDM}_\text{CVD} - \overline{\text{RDM}}_\text{HC}$, correlation distance, \citeNP{kriegeskorte2008}). It is downweighted to $0.2$ because the RDM and per-color vulnerability signals partially overlap.
$L_\text{smooth} = \tfrac{1}{8 \cdot 32400}\,\sum_c (\delta\theta_{c+1} - \delta\theta_c \mod \pm 180)^2$ is a circular-smoothness regularizer on the per-color shift vector $\delta\theta \in \mathbb{R}^8$ produced by the candidate $(\beta_s, \beta_c)$; it is downweighted to $0.1$ as a weak prior favouring smooth filters over discontinuous per-color shifts.

\paragraph{ROI scope.}
All four terms are computed at hV4 (V4 on disk).
We fit at hV4 rather than V1--V3 because hV4 is the only ROI in this dataset whose HC group LOCO interpolation exceeds the permutation null ($p = 0.044$ at hV4 vs.\ NS at V1/V2/V3; \nameref{sec:results:loco}), and because hV4 is the canonical cortical hub for hue representation in the ventral stream (\citeNP{brouwer2009, bannert2018, kuriki2015}); cone-shift parameters fit on an encoder that cannot interpolate held-out colors lack a voxel-level cone-shift interpretation.

\paragraph{Per-subject distortion fit.}
Parameters were searched by exhaustive grid over $\beta_s \in [0^\circ, 50^\circ]$ and $\beta_c \in [-50^\circ, 50^\circ]$ ($26 \times 51 = 1{,}326$ cells at $2^\circ$ resolution); $(\hat\beta_s, \hat\beta_c) = \arg\min L_\text{fit}$ was retained per CVD subject.
We checked the optimal distortion magnitude against an HC leave-one-out distribution under the same loss for validation (Supplementary~\ref{app:hc_perm}); quantitative filter validity is established by the Phase-3 2AFC behavioral arm (see the \nameref{sec:methods:behavioral} section, last paragraph).

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Stimulus-space filter}
\label{sec:methods:filter}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The subject-specific correction filter was derived as the pre-image of the fitted 2-component transform $T(\hat{\beta}_s, \hat{\beta}_c): \theta \mapsto \theta + \delta\theta(\theta;\hat{\beta}_s,\hat{\beta}_c)$.
For each of the 8 displayed hues $\theta_k$, we solved for the input angle $\tilde{\theta}_k$ such that $T(\tilde{\theta}_k) = \theta_k$, yielding a per-hue correction $\delta\theta_k^{\rm filt} = \tilde{\theta}_k - \theta_k$.
A CVD observer viewing $\tilde{\theta}_k$ thus receives, under the model, the same cortical hue representation as a healthy control observer viewing $\theta_k$.

Pre-images were computed by numerical inversion of the forward map (Brent's-method root-finding); inversion was exact ($|T(\tilde{\theta}_k) - \theta_k| < 0.001^\circ$) for all 8 hues in both CVD participants.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Behavioral concordance}
\label{sec:methods:behavioral}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Behavioral tasks were held on the same day as the MRI acquisition.

\paragraph{Just-noticeable difference (JND)}
Color discrimination thresholds were estimated for eight hue pairs using two-interleaved 1-up/1-down adaptive staircases, each terminating after eight reversals \cite{levitt1971}.
Pairs spanned three categories: (i) three pairs hypothesized as universally difficult for both CVD types (yellow--purple, blue--purple, red--orange), (ii) two pairs hypothesized as deutan-specific (orange--yellow, yellow--green), (iii) two pairs hypothesized as protan-specific (cyan--magenta, green--blue), and (iv) one control pair (red--cyan).

For each pair, the CVD JND threshold was compared to the HC distribution and assigned a behavioral impairment label: \emph{hypo} when the CVD threshold fell above the HC range (CVD/HC ratio $> 1$; discrimination worse than HC), or \emph{hyper} when it fell below (CVD/HC ratio $< 1$; discrimination preserved or better than HC). This pairwise hypo/hyper classification quantified where CVD behavior diverged from HC.

\paragraph{Simulator--JND concordance}
The fitted 2-component simulator was tested against the behavioral labels by comparing each pair's hypo/hyper classification with the simulator's predicted per-pair angular distortion under the fitted parameters $(\hat\beta_s, \hat\beta_c)$.
For a pair $(\theta_a, \theta_b)$, the simulator's predicted distortion magnitude was the sum of the per-color shifts applied to its two members, $|\delta\theta(\theta_a;\hat\beta_s,\hat\beta_c)| + |\delta\theta(\theta_b;\hat\beta_s,\hat\beta_c)|$.
The simulator and behavior were called concordant when hypo-labeled pairs corresponded to larger simulator-predicted distortions than hyper-labeled pairs, providing a direct neural-to-behavioral check independent of the fit loss.

\paragraph{Behavioral color identification}
An eight-alternative forced-choice (8AFC) color identification task was administered: participants performed six trials per color (48 trials per participant in total), and both the order of presented colors and the order of the eight answer options were randomized across trials. In parallel to the JND--simulator concordance above, each CVD response was evaluated against the simulator's predicted shifted angle: for a presented hue $\theta_k$, the fitted 2-component simulator predicts that responses should cluster near $\theta_k + \delta\theta(\theta_k;\hat\beta_s,\hat\beta_c)$. Exact and adjacent-bin agreement between observed responses and this predicted angle were tabulated as post-hoc consistency checks; quantitative validation is deferred to the Phase-3 2AFC arm.

\paragraph{Filter evaluation session (CVD only)}
CVD participants completed a second scanning session after the main experiment.
Three runs were acquired under the 2-component-derived stimulus-space filter and three runs under a generic Windows accessibility filter, presented in counterbalanced order.
LOCO decoding and LORO classification were repeated on second-session data to assess filter efficacy.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:methods:repro}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

All analyzes were conducted in Python 3.10 (numpy 1.24.3, scipy 1.11.3, scikit-learn 1.3.0, BrainIAK 0.11).
Random seeds were fixed (\texttt{seed=42}) for all permutation tests and bootstrap resampling.
Analysis code and preprocessing scripts are available at \url{https://github.com/Transconnectome/colorBlind_analysis}.
Anonymised preprocessed data are available on reasonable request to the corresponding author.
