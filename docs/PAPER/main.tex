\title{Distortion of cortical hue geometry in color vision deficiency enables an individualized color correction filter}
\shorttitle{Personalized cortical correction for CVD}
\author{Jinil Kim}
\affiliation{Seoul National University}

\keywords{color vision deficiency, anomalous trichromacy, fMRI, hV4, representational similarity analysis, forward encoding model, personalized color correction}

\abstract{
Color vision deficiency (CVD) is typically corrected with filters that target a population-average retina and treat every user alike. Yet the cortical color representation that perception reads out is reshaped individually, and whether it could guide a personalized correction is unknown. Here we show, using fMRI in two adults with CVD studied as single cases alongside healthy controls, that the cortical representation of color is not reduced in overall signal but distorted in its response structure. Both CVD participants discriminated all displayed hues across the visual hierarchy, yet the structure of their color representation was distorted in two complementary ways. Continuous interpolation between hues failed in area hV4, and the geometry of color representations itself was altered in early visual cortex (V1 and V2), differing between the two individuals. We then summarized each person's distortion with a compact two-component cortical model and analytically inverted it into a per-individual stimulus-space filter, designed to move the CVD color perception and cortical representation structure toward that of a healthy observer. In a second session in the deutan participant, the personalized filter moved the impaired cortical hue geometry back toward the healthy-control reference, where a deployed accessibility filter did not; both filters restored behavioral discrimination comparably, and validation in further individuals remains open. These findings recast CVD as an individually patterned distortion of cortical color structure rather than a loss of chromatic signal, one that can be inverted into a personalized correction.
}
