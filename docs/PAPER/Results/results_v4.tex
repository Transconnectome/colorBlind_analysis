% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Color discrimination is preserved across all ROIs in both CVD cases}
\label{sec:results:loro}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A corrective stimulus-space filter is valid only if the visual cortex of a CVD individual retains categorical discrimination among the target hues.
We verified this precondition using leave-one-run-out (LORO) cross-validation of the forward encoding model in SRM-aligned space.

Both CVD participants exceeded the 0.125 exact-accuracy chance level at every ROI (Figure~\ref{fig:loco}A).
A cross-subject generalization test of the same forward encoding model (HC-to-HC vs.\ HC-to-CVD pair-wise cross-decoding, all ROIs pooled) showed no HC--CVD difference (Mann--Whitney $U$ test, 21 HC-to-HC vs.\ 14 HC-to-CVD pairs, $p = 0.668$, rank-biserial effect size and exact test statistic in Supplementary~\ref{app:effect_sizes}).
The same null held at the within-ROI level at hV4 (Crawford \& Howell $t$-test, both CVD subjects within the HC range, $p = 0.142$).
The 8 scanner stimuli are therefore individually distinguishable at the cortical level in both CVD participants, in line with prior work on CVD above-threshold identification \cite{bosten2019, boehm2014}.
The representational substrate on which a corrective filter could act is present.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Color interpolation is impaired in both CVD cases}
\label{sec:results:loco}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

We next localized where continuous hue interpolation fails, which defines the functional target of the corrective transform, using leave-one-color-out (LOCO) decoding of the forward encoding model.
Among the four retinotopic ROIs, only hV4 supported above-chance hue interpolation in HC (adjacent accuracy $0.47 \pm 0.05$, mean $\pm$ SEM, $p = 0.044$ under $8! = 40{,}320$ exact label permutations).
V1--V3 did not exceed chance despite preserving cross-run discrimination.
This confirms hV4 as the primary interpolation gate, replicating \citeA{brouwer2009}.

Both CVD participants fell at or below the adjacent-accuracy chance level at hV4 (Figure~\ref{fig:loco}B).
Sub-08 reached adjacent accuracy $0.25$ (Crawford \& Howell $t = -1.58$, $p = 0.082$, n.s., $d_{cc} = -1.71$).
Sub-09 reached $0.13$ ($t = -2.48$, $p = 0.024$, $d_{cc} = -2.68$).
The impairment was not uniform across hues.
Both participants showed near-zero adjacent accuracy specifically for S-cone intermediate colors at hV4, namely blue, purple, and magenta (Figure~\ref{fig:loco}C).
Per-hue Crawford \& Howell tests reached significance for blue (sub-08 $d = 2.13$, $p = 0.047$; sub-09 $d = 2.15$, $p = 0.046$) and for purple (sub-08 $d = 2.40$, $p = 0.033$).
These per-hue accuracies form each participant's hue vulnerability profile $\mathbf{v} \in [0,1]^8$.

\subsection{Color geometry is distorted at a distinct ROI in each CVD case}
\label{sec:results:geometry}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

To characterize the geometric basis of the LOCO impairment, we compared the pairwise representational structure of CVD and HC participants in a shared SRM-aligned space.
For each CVD participant we computed disparity and the per-pair RDM deviation from the HC mean ($\Delta\text{RDM} = \text{RDM}_\text{CVD} - \overline{\text{RDM}}_\text{HC}$).

The ROI specificity of elevated disparity diverges between the two CVD participants, with V2-dominant disparity in deutan and V1-dominant disparity in protan (Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}B).
Sub-08 showed significantly elevated disparity specifically at V2 (Crawford \& Howell $p = 0.040$), with no significant elevation at V1, V3, or hV4.
Sub-09 showed significantly elevated disparity specifically at V1 ($p = 0.007$), with no significant elevation at V2, V3, or hV4.
Full test statistics and effect sizes ($t$, $d_{cc}$) for these Crawford--Howell comparisons are reported in Supplementary~\ref{app:effect_sizes}.

The $\Delta$RDM heatmaps (Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}A) reveal subject-specific distortion structures.
Sub-08 shows elevated distances among S-cone intermediate pairs at V2, and sub-09 shows a broader reorganization of pairwise distances at V1.
The ROIs of strongest disparity elevation (V1 for sub-09, V2 for sub-08) differ from the ROI used as the distortion-model loss target (hV4, see the \nameref{sec:methods:twocomp} section). Mean pairwise disparity is an existence statement about absolute geometric perturbation in early visual cortex. hV4 is the cortical stage at which the corrective filter must act, so the $\Delta$RDM enters the loss as a geometric convergence check at that ROI. V1/V2 disparity at upstream ROIs is parallel evidence of distortion rather than a competing fit target.

\subsection{A 2-component cortical distortion fits both CVD cases at hV4}
\label{sec:results:twocomp}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

To identify the distortion parameters needed for the corrective filter, we fit the 2-component cortical model (Equation~\ref{eq:2comp}) to each CVD participant's hV4 representation under the four-term neural-primary loss (Equation~\ref{eq:lfit}, see the \nameref{sec:methods:twocomp} section).

The fit returned $(\hat{\beta}_s, \hat{\beta}_c) = (38^\circ, -14^\circ)$ for sub-08 (deutan, $\|\hat{\beta}\| = 40.5^\circ$) and $(6^\circ, -22^\circ)$ for sub-09 (protan, $\|\hat{\beta}\| = 22.8^\circ$, Figure~\ref{fig:twocomp}).
The etiological asymmetry resides in the S-cone-axis magnitude ($\hat\beta_s = 38^\circ$ vs.\ $6^\circ$).
The confusion-axis rotation $\hat\beta_c$ operates along subtype-specific axes ($\theta_{\rm conf} = 150^\circ$ deutan, $16^\circ$ protan) and is not directly comparable in sign across subjects.
Both subjects show non-zero $\hat\beta_s$ along the S-cone axis on the same angular scale as the $21.4^\circ$ behavioral blue-yellow rotation reported in anomalous trichromacy by \citeA{emery2021}, confirming at the neural level the literature grounding adopted in Methods.

The fitted distortion is consistent with each subject's independent behavioral phenotype.
8AFC color identification placed both CVD subjects well below HC (HC $0.94 \pm 0.04$, sub-08 $0.71$, sub-09 $0.62$, chance $= 0.125$), with sub-08's errors clustering on the red--green axis (c2/c3 confused with c7, mean confusion $28\%$) and sub-09's errors extending across the L--M opponent range with additional magenta (c8) misidentification.

HC leave-one-out validation under the same loss is reported in Supplementary~\ref{app:hc_perm}, and the R+C cone-shift decomposition is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:altmodels}.
The HC label-permutation null is non-null for the 2-component class, so case-level distinctness rests on the joint pattern of vulnerability profile, R+C decomposition, and per-color filter direction rather than on $\|\hat\beta\|$ alone.

\subsection{\textcolor{red}{[TODO] Per-subject stimulus-space filter}}
\label{sec:results:filter}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

{\color{red}
The 2-component transform is bijective, so a stimulus-space pre-image $\tilde{\theta}_k$ such that $T(\tilde{\theta}_k) = \theta_k$ exists for every displayed hue $\theta_k$ (see the \nameref{sec:methods:filter} section).
The per-subject correction filter is $\delta\theta^{\rm filt}_k = \tilde{\theta}_k - \theta_k$.
For both participants, 8/8 pre-images were exact (residual $< 0.001^\circ$, Figure~\ref{fig:filter}).

For sub-08 (deutan, $\hat\beta_s = 38^\circ$, $\hat\beta_c = -14^\circ$, Stockman confusion axis $\theta_{\rm conf} = 150^\circ$), the filter applies a wide-amplitude L/M-rotation.
A near-uniform negative shift is applied across red, orange, yellow, green, and cyan ($\delta\theta^{\rm filt}$ ranging $-10^\circ$ to $-32^\circ$, peak $-32^\circ$ at cyan, $\theta = 180^\circ$).
A large opposite-sign rotation is applied at purple ($+29^\circ$, $\theta = 270^\circ$) and magenta ($+19^\circ$, $\theta = 315^\circ$).
The mean correction magnitude is $|\delta\theta^{\rm filt}| = 22^\circ$.

For sub-09 (protan, $\hat\beta_s = 6^\circ$, $\hat\beta_c = -22^\circ$, Stockman axis $\theta_{\rm conf} = 16^\circ$), the correction is a far smaller monotonic rotation along the protan confusion axis.
Peak displacements are at blue ($+21^\circ$, $\theta = 225^\circ$) and magenta ($-21^\circ$, $\theta = 315^\circ$), with near-zero shifts at green and cyan.
The mean correction magnitude is $|\delta\theta^{\rm filt}| = 10^\circ$.

The two correction signatures reflect distinct CVD etiologies rather than mirrored protan/deutan symmetry.
Under the R+C decomposition (Appendix~\ref{app:altmodels}), sub-08 is cortical-dominant and sub-09 is retinal-dominant.
A single retinal-family filter cannot serve both individuals.

The model-class expressiveness has a closed-form upper bound.
The magnitude $|\delta\theta|$ at $\theta = 45^\circ$ is bounded by $|0.71\,\hat\beta_s - 0.26\,\hat\beta_c|$.
Corrective shifts exceeding $\sim\!51^\circ$ at the deutan mid-axis (e.g.\ an orange-to-green excursion of $\gtrsim 85^\circ$) lie outside the 2-component class for any biologically plausible $\hat\beta$ and would require an additional mechanism such as saturation or lightness.
}
