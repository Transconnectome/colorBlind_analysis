\title{Distortion of cortical hue geometry in color vision deficiency enables an individualized color correction filter}
\shorttitle{Individualized cortical correction for CVD}
\author{Jinil Kim}
\affiliation{Seoul National University}

\keywords{color vision deficiency, anomalous trichromacy, fMRI, cortical color representation, individualized color correction}

\abstract{
Color vision deficiency (CVD) is typically corrected with generic filters designed from a retina-based model. Such filters change how colors appear but rarely improve discrimination. However, visual cortex, which is more directly tied to color perception, is overlooked in filter design, and how the cortical representation of color is reorganized in CVD is unknown. We hypothesized that by modeling an individual's cortical color representation, we could invert it into a personalized correction filter. Here we show, using fMRI in two adults, one with each red--green subtype of CVD, that the cortical color representation is not weaker in overall signal but is altered in its geometry, the relative positions of the colors within it. Decoding still separated all displayed colors in both CVD participants. Yet the continuous geometry relating those colors was distorted differently in the two individuals. We modeled each person's distortion as a hue rotation along two color-space axes and inverted the fit into a personalized stimulus-space filter. In a follow-up session, the individualized filter reduced discrimination errors (significantly in the deutan participant, at trend level in the protan) and introduced none, though this small sample cannot establish superiority over an existing filter. Whether it moved the cortical hue geometry toward the healthy reference differed between the two participants, and neither participant reached the reference. Establishing whether the filter produces a generalizable improvement will require validation in larger samples. This study identifies a previously uncharacterized geometric distortion of the cortical color representation in CVD and introduces, to our knowledge, the first framework for individualized, cortically grounded color correction. Systematic studies can quantify CVD's cortical distortions and provide a new class of personalized filters.
}
