\section*{Appendix A: Alternative Model Comparison}
\label{app:altmodels}

The main text adopts a 2-component cortical model (Section~\ref{sec:results:twocomp}). Here we compare it against the retinal-family alternative on the same held-out composite criterion (\S\ref{sec:methods:selection}), and give the structural reasons the retinal-family class is inadequate for the stimulus-space filter task.

\subsection*{A.1 Retinal-plus-cortical (R+C) fit on the composite criterion}

The retinal-plus-cortical model applies the subtype Machado cone-shift $\delta\theta_{\rm Mach}$ \citeNP{machado2009} scaled by a single cortical-gain parameter $g$ ($\delta\theta_{\rm RC} = (2 - g)\,\delta\theta_{\rm Mach}$; $g = 1$ retinal-only, $g = 2$ exact cortical cancellation; \citeNP{tregillus2021}). Scored on the same held-out composite as the 2-component model, R+C failed for both participants: for the deutan participant every resample saturated the gain boundary ($g \to 3.0$, $\langle 100\%\rangle$), so the fit was rejected as misspecified; for the protan participant the best fit ($g = 2.95$) generalized worse than the adopted 2-component model ($\overline{L}_{\rm test} = -0.86$ vs.\ $-1.54$). Full fits are in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:modelfits} and Section~\ref{sec:results:rc_insufficient}. A gain $g > 2$ implies cortical overshoot past the undistorted hue, inconsistent with the participants' confirmed residual CVD, so the saturated estimate is a model failure rather than a parameter value.

\subsection*{A.2 Inverse-mapping (pre-image) behavior}

The angular form of the 2-component model is bijective by construction: a stimulus-space pre-image $\theta + \delta\theta$ exists for any target hue. The retinal-shift component $\delta\theta_{\rm Mach}$ (the $g = 1$ retinal-only case), by contrast, compresses three hues (green $135^\circ$, cyan $180^\circ$, blue $225^\circ$) into a narrow ${\sim}26^\circ$ band (the eight shifted hues span only ${\sim}96^\circ$) at the protan participant's subtype severity, so they lose a unique pre-image and exact inversion fails at those positions. This arc-compression is a structural property of the retinal-shift form, independent of the fitting criterion, and disqualifies the retinal-family baseline for the stimulus-space filter task of Section~\ref{sec:results:filter}.

\subsection*{A.3 Why the cortical gain cannot recover the distortion}

Because the cortical gain $g$ rescales the retinal shift uniformly without generating the angular distortion captured by the 2-component model's $\beta_c \cos(\theta - \theta_{\rm conf})$ term, no value of $g$ matches the observed hV4 LOCO vulnerability pattern beyond what the retinal $\delta\theta_{\rm Mach}$ term alone provides. This single-axis degree-of-freedom deficit is the structural cause of the failure in A.1, and it holds across fitting criteria.
