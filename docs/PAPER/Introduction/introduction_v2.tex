\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

%==============================================================================
% Intro-1: What CVD is
%==============================================================================
Color vision deficiency (CVD) affects approximately 8\% of men and 0.4\% of women of European ancestry~\citep{birch2012}.
It arises from a shift in the spectral sensitivity of the L- or M-cone photopigment. The magnitude of that shift varies continuously across affected individuals~\citep{bosten2019}. X-linked polymorphisms in the cone opsin genes underlie the shift~\citep{deeb2005, neitz2011}. The two pigments lie between 1 and 12~nm apart in anomalous trichromacy, compared with roughly 27~nm in normal trichromacy~\citep{bosten2019, neitz2011}. This narrowed separation attenuates the L--M cone-opponent signal without abolishing it. Red--green CVD comprises two subtypes that differ in the direction of the shift. In protan observers the L-cone sensitivity moves toward the M cone, and in deutan observers the M-cone sensitivity moves toward the L cone~\citep{neitz2011}.

Observers who share a diagnostic label differ substantially in how much their color space is compressed. Anomalous trichromats show reduced red--green discrimination, frequent category confusions, and elevated just-noticeable differences along the L--M chromatic axis. The severity of these signs extends from near-normal to near-dichromatic within one diagnostic subtype~\citep{bosten2019, neitz2011}.

%==============================================================================
% Intro-2: Current CVD correction and its limits
%==============================================================================
Current CVD correction is calibrated to a population-average retina and applies the same transform to every user. Hardware filters are notch lenses. Each notch attenuates a narrow band of the spectrum where the L and M cones overlap. Removing that band widens the difference between the two cone signals~\citep{alvaro2022}. The EnChroma lens cuts three such notches at wavelengths taken from a normal retina~\citep{gomezrobledo2018}. The lens changes appearance without changing performance. Colors look more saturated and further apart along the red--green axis, while discrimination thresholds move minimally~\citep{somers2024}. The lens produces no clinically meaningful improvement on plate, arrangement, discrimination, naming or sorting tasks, and does not change diagnostic classification~\citep{gomezrobledo2018, alvaro2022}. Two commercial lenses shifted discrimination thresholds in 51 individuals with red--green CVD. For both lenses, variation between observers exceeded the mean effect~\citep{patterson2022}. Software filters start from the same population-average retina. They forward-project a stimulus onto a deficient retina, either by the Brettel--Vi\'enot--Mollon construction for dichromacy~\citep{brettel1997} or by the parameterized Machado model, which extends the same logic to anomalous trichromacy~\citep{machado2009}. Daltonization inverts these models. It recolors an image, moving red--green differences onto the blue--yellow and lightness axes~\citep{fidaner2005, simonliedtke2016}.

Individualized correction has been implemented at the retinal level. It tunes a cone-shift parameter to a user's responses on plate-style or appearance-matching tests. That parameter can be estimated accurately, from opsin genotype or from cone spectral sensitivities measured in observers of known genotype~\citep{deeb2005, stockman2000}. The retinal parameter predicts color discrimination imperfectly. Some anomalous trichromats with very small spectral separations discriminate better than their separation predicts~\citep{neitz2011}. Across observers, the correlation between cone spectral separation and color discrimination is weaker than expected~\citep{bosten2019}. A retinal parameter fixes the input to the visual system. It does not describe the cortical representation that is built from that input and determines color perception. Filters tuned to an observer's own cone sensitivities trade one set of confusions for another rather than moving color vision toward normal~\citep{gomezrobledo2018}. The remaining step is a filter derived from the user's own cortical color representation.

%==============================================================================
% Intro-3: Neural features of CVD
%==============================================================================
Cortical activity patterns specify both which color was seen and where that color lies among the others. Cortical color processing is distributed and hierarchical~\citep{gegenfurtner2003, shapley2011, conway2018}. Early visual cortex carries cone-opponent signals. In V1, perceptual hues form spatially clustered response patterns, while the cardinal directions do not~\citep{parkes2009}. V1 responses are also selective for hues that fall between the cone-opponent axes~\citep{kuriki2015}. In hV4, a hue withheld from training can be reconstructed from the responses to its neighbors~\citep{brouwer2009}. The relative positions of colors in these response patterns constitute a geometry, and a color can be identified in one observer from the response patterns of others~\citep{bannert2025}.

What has been measured in CVD is the strength of the color signal. Anomalous trichromats perceive more red--green contrast than a cone-sensitivity account predicts~\citep{boehm2014}. Deutan observers adapt to chromatic contrast more than the same account predicts~\citep{basim2025}. Both results are consistent with a post-receptoral gain that magnifies the weakened L--M signal. Long-term adaptation to the altered input can produce such an adjustment~\citep{webster2015}. In V1, color responses are weaker in anomalous trichromats than in normal trichromats. In ventral V2 and V3 they are indistinguishable~\citep{tregillus2021}.

Color deficiency also changes where colors lie relative to one another. Anomalous trichromats still describe stimuli with all four hue primaries, but each primary lands on a different stimulus than it does for normal trichromats~\citep{emery2021}. When dichromats judge the difference between color samples, red and green fall close together, and the map recovered from those judgments has one axis where normal trichromats have two~\citep{saysani2018}. When protan and deutan observers judge the difference between Munsell chips, the map keeps two axes but is dented at yellow and purple-blue. The size of that dent varies more than tenfold across observers, and some show no dent at all~\citep{ohkoba2021}. All of these are reports. The same relative positions have yet to be measured in the cortical response.

We take the individual's own cortical color representation as the reference and invert it into a correction on the stimulus. Among the regions that carry this geometry, only hV4 predicts behavior on a trial-by-trial basis in a color judgment task~\citep{bannert2018}. Our design adopts the premise that shifting an individual's hV4 color geometry toward that of the healthy controls moves their color perception with it.

%==============================================================================
% Intro-4: Gaps
%==============================================================================
Three gaps remain. \emph{(Gap 1)} The cortical color geometry of an individual with CVD remains to be described. Existing descriptions come from healthy observers and are reported at the group level~\citep{brouwer2009, brouwer2013, kuriki2015}. \emph{(Gap 2)} The distances between colors have not been measured in the cortical response in CVD. fMRI in CVD has reported response magnitude, including contrast-response gain in anomalous trichromacy~\citep{tregillus2021} and activation in single cases of dichromacy and achromatopsia~\citep{rina2024}. \emph{(Gap 3)} A filter inverted from an individual's own cortical representation remains underinvestigated. Existing designs invert retinal models.

%==============================================================================
% Intro-5: Research questions and hypotheses
%==============================================================================
We ask whether an individual's own cortical color representation can be turned into a correction for that person. We characterize two adults with CVD, one deutan and one protan, against a reference group of healthy controls (see Methods).

A stimulus-space filter is warranted and tractable in one specific regime, defined by two conditions. The displayed colors must remain decodable from the cortical response. This preserved decodability gives the filter a signal to act on. The continuous arrangement of hues must be distorted, so that there is something to correct. Our questions follow this logic.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Describe.} Does an individual's cortical hue geometry in CVD carry structured distortion, with the individual hues still decodable and their continuous arrangement impaired?
\item \textbf{Summarize.} Can a few interpretable parameters capture that distortion, treated as a compact description rather than as physiological measurements?
\item \textbf{Correct.} Can a realizable, individualized filter be derived from those parameters?
\item \textbf{Validate.} Does the filter move the cortical representation closer to the healthy-control reference than a deployed accessibility filter does?
\end{enumerate}

We expect the continuous arrangement of hues to be selectively impaired while the individual hues remain decodable. A few interpretable parameters should capture this distortion, and inverting them should yield a realizable, individualized correction.

Together, these steps yield a filter built for one individual. Its input is the cortical signal, its parameters are the fitted description, and its output is a stimulus shift. Each is defined at the level of the single observer, the level at which any correction must ultimately act. This study tests the feasibility of the inversion, namely whether an individual's cortical color geometry can be turned into a realizable filter. It does not test whether per-person correction outperforms a subtype average, which would require several individuals within one subtype.
