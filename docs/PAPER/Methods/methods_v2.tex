\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

The analysis follows three stages.
First, the fMRI neural signature of each CVD participant's hue-processing deficit was characterised in two ways: (1) leave-one-color-out (LOCO) decoding at hV4 yielding a hue vulnerability profile $\mathbf{v} \in [0,1]^8$ (see the \nameref{sec:methods:loco} section), and (2) representational geometry analysis quantifying pairwise hue dissimilarity structure via $\Delta$RDM (see the \nameref{sec:methods:rdm} section); both serve as fitting criteria for the distortion model.
Second, a two-component cortical distortion model fits these neural observations by simulating the joint effect of a retinal cone shift and a cortical orientation gain on HC-trained neural representations (see the \nameref{sec:methods:twocomp} section).
Third, the fitted distortion is analytically inverted to yield a stimulus-space pre-image filter that maps the distorted hue coordinates back towards the HC percept (see the \nameref{sec:methods:filter} section).

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Participants}
\label{sec:methods:participants}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Thirteen volunteers were recruited under Institutional Review Board approval (\#2510/002-023) and provided written informed consent.
Color vision was screened using the 14-plate Ishihara pseudoisochromatic test \cite{ishihara1917}.
Participants identifying $\geq 9$ plates correctly were classified as healthy controls (HC; $n = 7$, 3 female, age $22.7 \pm 2.5$ years).
Two male participants scoring below this threshold were classified as CVD: sub-08 (deuteranomalous, 5/14 plates corrected) and sub-09 (protanomalous, 7/14 plates corrected).
Four additional participants were excluded for failing to complete the full scanning protocol.
All CVD results are interpreted as single-case demonstrations using the \citeA{crawford1998} modified $t$-test; no population-level inference is made.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Stimuli and task}
\label{sec:methods:stimuli}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Eight isoluminant chromatic stimuli were drawn from CIE $L^{*}a^{*}b^{*}$ space at equal angular spacing ($0^\circ$--$315^\circ$, $45^\circ$ steps; $L^{*} = 75$, chroma $= 40$) \cite{cie1986}, spanning red, orange, yellow, green, cyan, blue, purple, and magenta (Figure~\ref{fig:paradigm}A).
A gray filler ($a^{*} = b^{*} = 0$) served as a null condition.

Stimuli were presented in a Rapid Serial Visual Presentation (RSVP) design to sustain attention and fixation.
A circular patch (1.5 s per stimulus) was shown in pseudo-random order optimised with Neurodesign \cite{durnez2018}.
Participants pressed a button upon detecting two consecutive 'K's within a rapid serial letter stream presented above the grating patch.
Each run contained eight repetitions of all eight chromatic stimuli and the gray filler; inter-stimulus intervals were drawn from $\{3, 4.5, 6\}$ s.
Each session comprised six runs ($\approx 7$ min each).
The experiment was coded in PsychoPy 2022.2.5 \cite{peirce2019}.

CVD participants additionally completed a second scanning session for filter evaluation (see the \nameref{sec:methods:behavioral} section).

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{MRI acquisition and preprocessing}
\label{sec:methods:mri}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Data were acquired on a Siemens 3T MAGNETOM Cima.X scanner with BioMatrix 3T coils.
To constrain coverage to posterior occipital cortex we used 24 oblique slices perpendicular to the calcarine sulcus \cite{brouwer2009} (TR 1.5 s, TE 30 ms, FA $75^\circ$, $2 \times 2 \times 2$ mm, $96 \times 80$ matrix).

Data were converted to BIDS format and defaced with ezBIDS \cite{levitas2024}.
Functional images were registered to the T1w image via mutual-information coregistration (FreeSurfer \texttt{mri\_coreg}; \citeNP{maes1997, wells1996}).
T1w-to-MNI normalisation used 12-DOF affine (FSL FLIRT; \citeNP{jenkinson2002}) followed by nonlinear warping (FNIRT; \citeNP{andersson2007}), yielding 2 mm isotropic MNI-space BOLD data.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{ROI definition and response estimation}
\label{sec:methods:roi}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Bilateral V1, V2, V3, and hV4 were defined from the Wang probabilistic atlas \cite{wang2015} at $> 50\%$ probability, intersected with each participant's BOLD brain mask (voxel counts: V1 $655 \pm 214$; V2 $451 \pm 145$; V3 $103 \pm 29$; hV4 $63 \pm 22$; mean $\pm$ SD).

Single-trial response amplitudes were estimated with a two-stage GLM \cite{dale1999, brouwer2009, brouwer2013}.
First, an ROI-level hemodynamic response function (HRF) was extracted from a finite-impulse-response (FIR) model (8-TR basis set).
Voxels in the top 50\% of variance explained by the mean ROI HRF were retained (V1 $328 \pm 107$; V2 $226 \pm 73$; V3 $52 \pm 15$; hV4 $32 \pm 11$ voxels).
Second, the estimated HRF was convolved with per-color design matrices and fitted by ridge regression to yield one amplitude per (voxel, color, run), organized as a $6 \times 8 \times V$ array per subject.

Voxel amplitudes were aligned across runs using within-subject Procrustes rotation \cite{gower1975}: runs 2--6 were mapped to run 1 via the orthogonal transformation minimising $\|X_\text{ref} - XQ\|_F$ (scaling prohibited).

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Shared Response Model}
\label{sec:methods:srm}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Cross-subject alignment and dimensionality reduction were performed with the Shared Response Model (SRM; \citeNP{chen2015}).
SRM estimates per-participant orthonormal mappings $W_i \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times k}$ ($V$ voxels, $k$ latent dimensions) and a group-shared response matrix $S \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times 8}$:

\begin{equation}
  \min_{W_i,\,S}\;\sum_{i=1}^N \|X_i - W_i S\|_F^2 \quad\text{s.t.}\quad W_i^\top W_i = I_k
\end{equation}

SRM was trained exclusively on the seven HC participants to avoid circular inference in CVD comparisons.
Each CVD participant was subsequently projected into the fixed HC-derived space by solving $W_\text{CVD} = UV^\top$ where $U\Sigma V^\top = \text{SVD}(X_\text{CVD}\cdot S^\dagger)$.
Reduced dimensions were chosen by mean-rank aggregation across seven-fold leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) cross-validation, using three SRM-quality metrics (RDM split-half reliability, cross-subject RDM correlation, and reconstruction error): V1 $k = 4$; V2 $k = 4$; V3 $k = 3$; hV4 $k = 3$.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Forward encoding model}
\label{sec:methods:encoding}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Both discrimination (LORO) and interpolation (LOCO) decoding used the same forward encoding model \cite{brouwer2009}.
The model represents each stimulus as a $F = 6$-dimensional channel vector $\mathbf{c}(\theta) = [c_1(\theta), \ldots, c_F(\theta)]^\top$, where $c_k(\theta) = \max\!\bigl(0,\,\cos(\theta - \theta_k)\bigr)^2$ is a half-wave rectified squared cosine tuned to preferred hue $\theta_k$ (preferred hues spaced equally at $60^\circ$; \citeNP{brouwer2009}).
A weight matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{F \times V}$ is estimated by ridge regression with cross-validated regularization (\texttt{RidgeCV}; \citeNP{pedregosa2011}) to predict voxel amplitudes from channel activations: $\hat{X} = CW^\top$.
Decoding inverts the encoding direction: for a held-out voxel pattern $\mathbf{x}$, channel activations are reconstructed as $\hat{\mathbf{c}} = W\mathbf{x}$, and the candidate hue whose basis vector has the highest Pearson correlation with $\hat{\mathbf{c}}$ is returned as the decoded colour.
Alternative decoders evaluated during model selection are described in Appendix~\ref{app:altmodels}.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Discrimination decoding (LORO)}
\label{sec:methods:loro}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Categorical discrimination was assessed with leave-one-run-out (LORO) cross-validation in SRM-aligned space using the forward encoding model (see the \nameref{sec:methods:encoding} section).
For each of the six runs, $W$ was estimated on the remaining five runs using mean-subtracted SRM projections, and each held-out run's 8 colour patterns were decoded by nearest-neighbour matching.
Primary discrimination performance was the 8-class exact accuracy (acc\_exact; chance $= 1/8 = 0.125$), averaged across six folds.

Group-level HC--CVD comparison used a Mann--Whitney $U$ test on cross-subject generalization scores: the encoding model trained on each HC participant's SRM-aligned data was applied to every other participant's aligned patterns, yielding 21 unique HC-to-HC and 14 HC-to-CVD pairwise generalization scores.
Within-ROI single-case tests used the \citeA{crawford1998} modified $t$-test (one-tailed, lower).

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Interpolation decoding (LOCO)}
\label{sec:methods:loco}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Continuous hue interpolation was assessed with leave-one-color-out (LOCO) cross-validation using the same forward encoding model (see the \nameref{sec:methods:encoding} section).
Each of the 8 hues was held out in turn; $W$ was estimated on the remaining 7 hues using all 6 runs (pooled $42$-sample design matrix), and the held-out hue was predicted by nearest-neighbour matching in channel-output space.

\paragraph{Adjacent accuracy}
The primary LOCO metric was adjacent accuracy (adj\_acc): the proportion of predictions that fell within $\pm 1$ hue step ($45^\circ$) of the target hue, averaged across the 6 runs of each test color (range $0$--$1$; chance level under a uniform random predictor $= 3/8 = 0.375$; exact-accuracy chance $= 1/8 = 0.125$).
For HC above-chance testing and for pairwise HC--CVD comparisons at hV4, $p$-values were obtained from 1,000 random color-label permutations and from the \citeA{crawford1998} modified $t$-test (one-tailed, lower), respectively.

\paragraph{Vulnerability profile}
The 8-dimensional vector of per-hue adj\_acc values, $\mathbf{v} \in [0,1]^8$, constitutes each participant's \emph{hue vulnerability profile}.
For group-level model fit, we report Spearman $\rho$ between the distortion-model-predicted and observed $\mathbf{v}$ (see the \nameref{sec:methods:twocomp} section).
Per-hue group comparisons (Figure~\ref{fig:loco}C) used the \citeA{crawford1998} modified $t$-test (one-tailed, uncorrected).
Effect sizes are reported as Hedges' $d$ \cite{hedges1985} with the Crawford--Howell correction for small-$n$ comparisons.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{$\Delta$RDM analysis}
\label{sec:methods:rdm}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

To characterise the geometric basis of LOCO impairment we computed two measures in SRM-aligned space: mean pairwise disparity (absolute elevation) and $\Delta$RDM (directional structure).

\paragraph{Pairwise disparity}
For each participant and ROI, the representational dissimilarity matrix (RDM) was computed as the $8 \times 8$ matrix of pairwise correlation distances ($1 - \text{Pearson}\,r$).
Mean pairwise disparity was defined as the average of all 28 upper-triangle elements.
HC disparity was estimated with a leave-one-subject-out (LOO) procedure to remove the within-subject diagonal bias.
Individual CVD disparity was compared to the HC LOO distribution using the \citeA{crawford1998} modified $t$-test (one-tailed, upper; significance at $p < 0.05$).

\paragraph{$\Delta$RDM}
The deviation of CVD representational geometry from the HC mean was quantified as $\Delta\text{RDM} = \text{RDM}_\text{CVD} - \overline{\text{RDM}}_ \text{HC,LOO}$, where $\overline{\text{RDM}}_\text{HC,LOO}$ is the HC mean RDM excluding the left-out subject. $\Delta$RDM was visualised as a heatmap over all 28 color pairs (Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}A).
To test whether a cone-shift model predicts the observed $\Delta$RDM structure, we computed the Spearman $\rho$ between the upper-triangle of the predicted $\Delta\text{RDM}_\text{model}$ and the observed $\Delta\text{RDM}$, and assessed significance against 1,000 random sign-flip permutations of the upper-triangle elements.

Pairwise-disparity tests (Crawford \& Howell) and $\Delta$RDM permutation tests address different null hypotheses: the former tests whether absolute pairwise distances are elevated; the latter tests whether a specific distortion model accounts for the \emph{structure} of those deviations.
A significant result under one test does not imply significance under the other.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Two-component distortion model}
\label{sec:methods:twocomp}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CVD hue distortion was modelled as a two-component angular shift in Stockman opponent-color space:

\begin{equation}
  \delta\theta(h;\,\beta_s,\beta_c) = \beta_s \cos(h - 90^\circ) + \beta_c \cos(h - \theta_{\rm conf})
  \label{eq:2comp}
\end{equation}

where $h$ is the opponent-hue angle of the stimulus in CIELab space and $\theta_{\rm conf}$ is the CVD-subtype confusion axis ($16^\circ$ protan; $150^\circ$ deutan), derived from the Brettel--Vi\'enot--Mollon dichromatic simulation \cite{brettel1997} applied to Stockman \& Sharpe LMS cone fundamentals \cite{stockman2000}. $\beta_s$ parameterises distortion along the S-cone axis ($h = 90^\circ$), motivated by cortical-RDM evidence that CVD errors concentrate at S-cone loci \cite{emery2021}. $\beta_c$ parameterises rotation along the confusion axis, with both signs unconstrained as free parameters of the fit.

Per-subject parameters $(\beta_s, \beta_c)$ were recovered by minimising a four-term, neural-primary loss derived from the V4 LOCO forward model:

\begin{equation}
  L_\text{fit}(\beta_s, \beta_c) = 1.0\,L_\text{vuln} + 0.5\,L_\text{rank} + 0.2\,L_\text{rdm} + 0.1\,L_\text{smooth}
  \label{eq:lfit}
\end{equation}

with each term normalised to $[0,1]$ before weighting.
Let $\mathbf{v}_\text{sim}(\beta_s, \beta_c) = f_\text{forward}\!\bigl(C(\theta + \delta\theta(\,\cdot\,;\beta_s,\beta_c));\,W\bigr) \in [0,1]^8$ be the simulated per-colour V4 LOCO vulnerability ($1 - \rho$) obtained by re-applying the HC-trained ridge-GCV encoding weights $W$ to the design matrix $C$ shifted by the candidate distortion, and let $\mathbf{v}_\text{obs} \in [0,1]^8$ be the observed CVD per-colour vulnerability.

\paragraph{Loss-term roles.}
$L_\text{vuln} = \tfrac{1}{8 \cdot 4}\,\|\mathbf{v}_\text{sim} - \mathbf{v}_\text{obs}\|^2$ (per-colour MSE, normaliser $4.0$ for $[0,1]$ amplitude range) is the primary fit term: it forces the simulated vulnerability profile to match the per-colour LOCO breakdown that the CVD encoder exhibits, which is the operative neural quantity for the corrective filter (see the \nameref{sec:discussion} section for the LOCO--behaviour concordance evidence underlying this choice).
$L_\text{rank} = (1 - \rho_\text{Spearman}(\mathbf{v}_\text{sim}, \mathbf{v}_\text{obs}))/2$ is an ordering term that breaks ties when the MSE landscape is locally flat by penalising rank-reordering of vulnerable colours; it is downweighted to $0.5$ because amplitude (MSE) is the operative signal.
$L_\text{rdm} = (1 - \cos(\Delta\text{RDM}_\text{sim}, \Delta\text{RDM}_\text{obs}))/2$ is a geometric convergence check on the 28-pair upper-triangle of the CVD-minus-HC representational dissimilarity-matrix difference at hV4 ($\Delta\text{RDM} = \text{RDM}_\text{CVD} - \overline{\text{RDM}}_\text{HC,LOO}$, correlation distance; \citeNP{kriegeskorte2008}); it is downweighted to $0.2$ because the RDM and per-colour vulnerability signals partially overlap.
$L_\text{smooth} = \tfrac{1}{8 \cdot 32400}\,\sum_c (\delta\theta_{c+1} - \delta\theta_c \mod \pm 180)^2$ is a circular-smoothness regulariser on the per-colour shift vector $\delta\theta \in \mathbb{R}^8$ produced by the candidate $(\beta_s, \beta_c)$; it is downweighted to $0.1$ as a weak prior favouring smooth filters over discontinuous per-colour shifts.

\paragraph{ROI scope.}
All four terms are computed at hV4 (V4 on disk).
We fit at hV4 rather than V1--V3 because hV4 is the only ROI in this dataset whose HC group LOCO interpolation exceeds the permutation null ($p = 0.044$ at hV4 vs.\ NS at V1/V2/V3; \nameref{sec:results:loco}), and because hV4 is the canonical cortical hub for hue representation in the ventral stream (\citeNP{brouwer2009, bannert2018, kuriki2015}); cone-shift parameters fit on an encoder that cannot interpolate held-out colours lack a voxel-level cone-shift interpretation.

\paragraph{Search and HC-LOO calibration.}
Parameters were searched by exhaustive grid over $\beta_s \in [0^\circ, 50^\circ]$ and $\beta_c \in [-50^\circ, 50^\circ]$ ($26 \times 51 = 1{,}326$ cells at $2^\circ$ resolution); $(\hat\beta_s, \hat\beta_c) = \arg\min L_\text{fit}$ was retained per subject.
Statistical descriptors are reported per subject as: (i) label-permutation $p_\text{perm}$ (1,000 permutations of the 8-colour label assignment) for fit validity on real CVD data, and (ii) HC leave-one-out descriptive refit, where each of $n = 6$ effective HC participants (excluding sub-07 for low hV4 voxel count) is refit under the same loss with the remaining HC pool as reference, yielding a descriptive HC $\|\hat\beta\|$ distribution.
Per-subject p-values are descriptive of representational-geometry fit, not specificity statements: the HC label-permutation false-positive rate at hV4 under Equation~\ref{eq:lfit} reaches $7/7$ (see Supplementary~\ref{app:hc_perm} and the \nameref{sec:discussion} section for the statistical-framework caveats).

The 2-component class has an angular expressiveness limit $|\delta\theta|(\theta\!=\!45^\circ) = |0.71\,\beta_s - 0.26\,\beta_c|$, so hue translations exceeding $\sim\!51^\circ$ at the deutan mid-axis (e.g.\ orange $\to$ green) lie outside the model class for any biologically plausible $\beta$ (see the \nameref{sec:results:twocomp} section).
Comparison with alternative model classes (1-DOF Machado cone-shift; 2-DOF retinal+cortical) is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:altmodels}.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Stimulus-space filter}
\label{sec:methods:filter}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The subject-specific correction filter was derived as the pre-image of the fitted 2-component transform $T(\hat{\beta}_s, \hat{\beta}_c): \theta \mapsto \theta + \delta\theta(\theta;\hat{\beta}_s,\hat{\beta}_c)$.
For each of the 8 displayed hues $\theta_k$, we solved for the input angle $\tilde{\theta}_k$ such that $T(\tilde{\theta}_k) = \theta_k$, yielding a per-hue correction $\delta\theta_k^{\rm filt} = \tilde{\theta}_k - \theta_k$.
A CVD observer viewing $\tilde{\theta}_k$ thus receives, under the model, the same cortical hue representation as a healthy control observer viewing $\theta_k$.

Pre-images were computed numerically: 1,440 candidate values were evaluated on the forward map, a sign-change bracket was identified, and Brent's method was applied for refinement.
Inversion was verified by $|T(\tilde{\theta}_k) - \theta_k| < 0.001^\circ$ for all 8 hues and both CVD participants.
Behaviour of alternative model classes under pre-image inversion is detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:altmodels}.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Behavioral concordance}
\label{sec:methods:behavioral}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{Just-noticeable difference (JND)}
Color discrimination thresholds were estimated for eight hue pairs using two-interleaved 1-up/1-down adaptive staircases, each terminating after eight reversals \cite{levitt1971}.
Pairs spanned three categories: (i) three pairs hypothesised as universally difficult for both CVD types (yellow--purple, blue--purple, red--orange), (ii) two pairs hypothesised as deutan-specific (orange--yellow, yellow--green), (iii) two pairs hypothesised as protan-specific (cyan--magenta, green--blue), and (iv) one control pair (red--cyan).
LOCO--JND concordance was assessed by asking whether each pair's LOCO-vulnerability classification (above vs.\ below HC range) agreed with the JND-impairment classification (above vs.\ below HC range).

\paragraph{8AFC color identification}
Participants selected the matching hue from eight options identical to the scanner stimulus set.
Same-day identifications were compared to Ishihara classifications post-hoc.

\paragraph{Filter evaluation session (CVD only)}
CVD participants completed a second scanning session after the main experiment.
Three runs were acquired under the 2-component-derived stimulus-space filter and three runs under a generic Windows accessibility filter, presented in counterbalanced order.
LOCO decoding and LORO classification were repeated on second-session data to assess filter efficacy.

\paragraph{Planned validation}
A two-alternative forced-choice (2AFC) discrimination arm targeting filter-induced perceptual restoration is pre-registered for Phase 3 (data not collected at the time of writing).
