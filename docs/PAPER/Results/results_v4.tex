\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Participants and behavioral phenotype}
\label{sec:results:participants}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Two CVD participants spanning the deutan--protan dichotomy were recruited
alongside seven healthy controls (HC; sub-01--07). Sub-08 (male) was
classified as mild--moderate deuteranomalous (8/14 Ishihara plates misread;
deutan axis confirmed by protan--deutan discrimination item);
Sub-09 (male) as moderate--severe protanomalous (11/14 plates misread;
protan axis). Same-day 8-alternative forced-choice (8AFC) identification
of the eight scanner hue stimuli confirmed the Ishihara classification
(HC: $0.94 \pm 0.04$; Sub-08: $0.71$; Sub-09: $0.62$; chance $= 0.125$).
Sub-08's errors clustered on the red--green axis (c2/c3 vs.\ c7;
mean confusion $28\%$); Sub-09's errors extended across the L--M
opponent range with additional misidentification of magenta (c8).
Because only two symptomatic CVD cases were available, all inferences
are framed as single-case analyses following \citeA{crawford1998}
and \citeA{schuett2023}; no population-level CVD claim is made.
The experimental paradigm and full analysis pipeline are summarised in
Figure~\ref{fig:paradigm}.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Color discrimination is preserved across all ROIs in CVD}
\label{sec:results:loro}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A corrective stimulus-space filter is meaningful only if the visual cortex
of a CVD individual retains categorical discrimination among the target
hues. We verified this precondition using leave-one-run-out (LORO)
cross-validation of a linear discriminant classifier applied to
SRM-aligned responses.

Both CVD participants exceeded the 0.125 exact-accuracy chance level at
every ROI (Figure~\ref{fig:loco}A). A cross-subject LDA generalization
test (HC-to-HC vs.\ HC-to-CVD pair-wise cross-decoding, all ROIs pooled)
showed no HC--CVD difference ($p = 0.668$, Mann--Whitney $U$,
28 HC-to-HC vs.\ 12 HC-to-CVD pairs). The same null held at the
within-ROI level at hV4 (Crawford \& Howell $t$-test: both CVD subjects
within the HC range; $p = 0.142$). In line with prior work on CVD
above-threshold identification \cite{bosten2019, boehm2014},
the 8 scanner stimuli are individually distinguishable at the cortical
level in both CVD participants; the representational substrate on which
a corrective filter could act is present.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{hV4 color interpolation is selectively impaired in CVD}
\label{sec:results:loco}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

We next asked where continuous hue geometry fails --- the functional target
of the corrective transform. Leave-one-colour-out (LOCO) decoding trains
the encoder on 7 of 8 hues and measures whether the held-out hue is
predicted from the remaining representational structure, yielding an
8-dimensional vulnerability profile per participant.

Among the four retinotopic ROIs, only hV4 supported above-chance hue
interpolation in HC (adjacent accuracy $\text{adj\_acc} = 0.47 \pm 0.05$,
mean $\pm$ SEM; $p_\text{perm} = 0.044$, label permutation; V1--V3
did not exceed chance despite preserving LORO discrimination). This
confirms hV4 as the primary interpolation gate, replicating
\citeA{brouwer2009}.

Both CVD participants fell at or below the adjacent-accuracy chance level
at hV4 (Sub-08: $\text{adj\_acc} = 0.25$, Crawford \& Howell $t = -1.58$,
$p = 0.082$, n.s.; Sub-09: $\text{adj\_acc} = 0.13$, $t = -2.48$,
$p = 0.024$; Figure~\ref{fig:loco}B). The impairment was not uniform
across hues: both participants showed near-zero adjacent accuracy
specifically for S-cone intermediate colors (blue, purple, magenta at
hV4; per-hue Crawford \& Howell tests: blue, Sub-08 $d = 2.13$,
$p = 0.047$; Sub-09 $d = 2.15$, $p = 0.046$; purple, Sub-08
$d = 2.40$, $p = 0.033$; Figure~\ref{fig:loco}C). This per-hue
impairment pattern is consistent with both participants' behavioral
error distributions from the 8AFC task and with the JND confusion pairs
identified in the pre-scanner session (LOCO--JND concordance 100\%, 6/6
pairs; SRM geometry--JND concordance 33\%, 2/6 pairs), establishing
LOCO as the functional phenotype that predicts behavioral color
discrimination failure.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{CVD color representation geometry is distorted in a
  subject-specific, ROI-specific pattern}
\label{sec:results:geometry}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

To characterise the geometric basis of the LOCO impairment, we compared
the pairwise representational structure of CVD and HC participants in a
shared SRM-aligned space. For each CVD participant we computed the RDM
difference ($\Delta\text{RDM} = \text{RDM}_\text{CVD} -
\overline{\text{RDM}}_\text{HC,LOO}$) and the mean pairwise correlation
distance (disparity).

Sub-08 showed significantly elevated disparity specifically at V2
(Crawford \& Howell $p = 0.040$), with no significant elevation at V1,
V3, or hV4. Sub-09 showed significantly elevated disparity specifically
at V1 ($p = 0.007$). Sub-10 (near-normal deutan, included as a within-study
control) showed no significant elevation at any ROI (all $p > 0.43$),
consistent with the null behavioral phenotype. The ROI specificity
diverges between the two CVD participants --- V2-dominant for deutan,
V1-dominant for protan --- and is inconsistent with a shared group-level
gain mechanism (Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}B).

The $\Delta$RDM heatmaps (Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}A) reveal
subject-specific distortion structures: Sub-08 shows elevated distances
among S-cone intermediate pairs at V2; Sub-09 shows a broader
reorganisation of pairwise distances at V1. A permutation test of the
retinal+cortical (R+C) cone-shift model against the observed $\Delta$RDM
was significant for Sub-09 at V1 ($p = 0.026$) but not Sub-08 at V2
($p = 0.179$), indicating that geometric distortion measured at the
population-code level is complementary to, not redundant with, the
LOCO-based functional characterisation.

\subsection{A two-component model captures individual CVD hV4 LOCO
  vulnerability}
\label{sec:results:twocomp}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

To identify the distortion parameters needed to build a corrective filter,
we fitted three nested models to each participant's hV4 LOCO vulnerability
profile by grid search: (i) the 1-DOF Machado cone shift ($\Delta\lambda$;
\citeNP{machado2009}), (ii) the 2-DOF cortical 2-component model
($\beta_s$ for S-cone axis, $\beta_c$ for confusion-axis rotation), and
(iii) the 2-DOF retinal+cortical R+C model ($\Delta\lambda, g$;
\citeNP{tregillus2021}).

The 2-component model reached significance for both participants (Sub-08:
$\beta_s = 38^\circ$, $\beta_c = -14^\circ$, Spearman $\rho = 0.88$,
$p_\text{perm} = 0.004$; Sub-09: $\beta_s = 6^\circ$, $\beta_c = -22^\circ$,
$\rho = 0.69$, $p = 0.035$; 40,320 permutations;
Figure~\ref{fig:twocomp}B). Machado reached significance for Sub-09
($\Delta\lambda = 13.5$ nm, $\rho = 0.76$, $p = 0.018$) but not for
Sub-08 ($\rho = 0.62$, $p = 0.058$, n.s.). The 2-component model is
preferred for both subjects because it yields exact pre-image solutions
for all 8 hues (see Section~\ref{sec:results:filter}), whereas Machado
produces arc compression that prevents exact inversion for Sub-09.

The parameter landscape (Figure~\ref{fig:twocomp}C) reveals a clear
ridge structure: $\beta_s$ and $\beta_c$ trade off along a diagonal
for each subject, with distinct optimal locations. The $\beta_s$ values
($38^\circ$ for Sub-08; $6^\circ$ for Sub-09) are consistent in range
with the $21.4^\circ$ S-cone phase shift reported by
\citeA{emery2021} in an independent anomalous-trichromat cohort
assessed via behavioral hue scaling; however, direct numerical
comparison is not warranted as the quantities are defined on different
physical scales (degrees of DKL angular rotation vs.\ behavioral scaling
units). The $\beta_s$ values serve here as physiological grounding for
the model structure, not as parameter-value convergence claims.

\subsection{Personalized stimulus-space filters are exact and individually
  distinct}
\label{sec:results:filter}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

We computed the per-hue correction filter as the stimulus-space pre-image
of the 2-component distortion. For each of the 8 displayed hues we solved
for the input angle $\theta_\text{in}$ such that the 2-component forward
map produces the target hue $\theta_\text{target}$, yielding a correction
vector $\delta\theta \in \mathbb{R}^8$ per participant.

Both subjects yielded exact pre-images for all 8 hues (maximum residual
$< 0.001^\circ$; Figure~\ref{fig:filter}A). The per-subject mean correction
magnitudes differ substantially: Sub-08 requires a mean shift of $46.3^\circ$
(maximum $104.2^\circ$ at cyan); Sub-09 requires $20.1^\circ$ (maximum
$48.1^\circ$ at orange), a $2.3\times$ difference in correction scale.

For comparison, the Machado pre-image for Sub-09 collapses three hues
(green $135^\circ$, cyan $180^\circ$, blue $225^\circ$) onto a single
pre-image angle ($\sim 127^\circ$), preventing exact inversion (4/8 exact;
Figure~\ref{fig:filter}B). The 2-component model, whose inverse is
guaranteed to exist by construction (bijective angular dilation), resolves
this arc compression and yields exact corrections at all 8 hue positions.

The two subjects' correction profiles differ not only in magnitude but
also in sign at 4 of 8 hue positions (cyan--magenta arc, hues 5--8;
Figure~\ref{fig:filter}C; sub-08 vs.\ sub-09 filter cosine similarity
$= 0.55$). A correction optimised for one participant's deutan geometry
would move orange, cyan, blue, purple, and magenta in the \emph{wrong}
direction for the other participant's protan geometry. This divergence
directly motivates the individual-derivation approach and disqualifies
any group-average correction for this pair of subjects.
