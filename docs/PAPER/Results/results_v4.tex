\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Color discrimination is preserved across all ROIs in both CVD cases}
\label{sec:results:loro}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A corrective stimulus-space filter is valid only if the visual cortex of a CVD individual retains categorical discrimination among the target hues. We verified this precondition using leave-one-run-out (LORO) cross-validation of the forward encoding model in SRM-aligned space.

Both CVD participants exceeded the 0.125 exact-accuracy chance level at every ROI (Figure~\ref{fig:loco}A). A cross-subject generalization test of the same forward encoding model (HC-to-HC vs.\ HC-to-CVD pair-wise cross-decoding, all ROIs pooled) showed no HC--CVD difference (Mann--Whitney $U$ test, 21 HC-to-HC vs.\ 14 HC-to-CVD pairs, $p = 0.668$, rank-biserial effect size and exact test statistic in Supplementary~\ref{app:effect_sizes}). The same null held at the within-ROI level at hV4 (Crawford \& Howell $t$-test, both CVD subjects within the HC range, $p = 0.142$). The 8 scanner stimuli are therefore individually distinguishable at the cortical level in both CVD participants, in line with prior work on CVD above-threshold identification \cite{boehm2014, bosten2019}.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Color interpolation is impaired in both CVD cases}
\label{sec:results:loco}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

We next tested whether continuous hue interpolation fails, which indicated structural impairment in cortical representation, using leave-one-color-out (LOCO) decoding of the forward encoding model. Among the four retinotopic ROIs, only hV4 supported above-chance hue interpolation in HC (adjacent accuracy $0.47 \pm 0.05$, mean $\pm$ SEM over $n = 6$ HC, sub-07 excluded at hV4 for insufficient voxels; $p = 0.044$ under $8! = 40{,}320$ exact label permutations). V1--V3 did not exceed chance despite preserving cross-run discrimination. This is consistent with hV4 as the primary interpolation gate, replicating \citeA{brouwer2009}.

Both CVD participants fell at or below the adjacent-accuracy chance level at hV4 (Figure~\ref{fig:loco}B). Sub-08 reached adjacent accuracy $0.25$ (Crawford \& Howell $t = -1.58$, $p = 0.082$, n.s., $d_{cc} = -1.71$). Sub-09 reached $0.13$ ($t = -2.48$, $p = 0.024$, $d_{cc} = -2.68$). The impairment was not uniform across hues. Both participants showed near-zero adjacent accuracy at hV4 for the S-cone intermediate colors blue, purple, and magenta (adjacent accuracy $= 0$ for both participants at all three hues; Figure~\ref{fig:loco}C). Per-hue Crawford \& Howell tests (one-tailed) reached significance only for blue (both participants $d = 2.20$, $p = 0.042$); purple ($d = 1.02$, $p = 0.19$) and magenta ($d = 1.89$, $p = 0.064$) did not survive the single-case test against the $n = 6$ HC distribution. These per-hue accuracies form each participant's hue vulnerability profile $\mathbf{v} \in [0,1]^8$.

\subsection{Color geometry is distorted at a distinct ROI in each CVD case}
\label{sec:results:geometry}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

To characterize the geometric basis of the LOCO impairment, we compared the pairwise representational structure of CVD and HC participants in a shared SRM-aligned space. For each CVD participant we computed disparity and the per-pair RDM deviation from the HC mean ($\Delta\text{RDM} = \text{RDM}_\text{CVD} - \overline{\text{RDM}}_\text{HC}$).

The ROI of elevated disparity differs between the two CVD participants: V2 in sub-08 and V1 in sub-09 (Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}B). Sub-08 showed significantly elevated disparity specifically at V2 (Crawford \& Howell $p = 0.040$), with no significant elevation at V1, V3, or hV4. Sub-09 showed significantly elevated disparity specifically at V1 ($p = 0.007$), with no significant elevation at V2, V3, or hV4. Full test statistics and effect sizes ($t$, $d_{cc}$) for these Crawford--Howell comparisons are reported in Supplementary~\ref{app:effect_sizes}.

The $\Delta$RDM heatmaps (Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}A) reveal subject-specific distortion structures. Sub-08 shows elevated distances among S-cone intermediate pairs at V2, and sub-09 shows a broader reorganization of pairwise distances at V1. Disparity tests the absolute amplitude of pairwise distances against the HC leave-one-out distribution, whereas the pairwise $\Delta$RDM shows the directional pattern of those deviations; the significant V1/V2 disparity is independent evidence that the cortical color representation is geometrically distorted.

\subsection{The retinal-plus-gain model is structurally insufficient}
\label{sec:results:rc_insufficient}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The R+C model is structurally insufficient for both CVD participants. Fitting this model to each CVD participant yielded ceiling saturation in both cases.
For sub-08, $\langle 100\%\rangle$ of resample solutions saturated the grid boundary ($g = 3.0$).
For sub-09, saturation remained high ($\langle 41\%\rangle$, $g = 2.95$).
Boundary saturation at these rates indicates model misspecification, not a well-constrained optimum \cite{wilson2019}.

A gain $g > 2$ implies that cortical processing reverses the direction of the retinal shift past the undistorted hue (Section~\ref{sec:methods:candidates}). Both participants scored below the Ishihara criterion (sub-08: 5/14 plates correct; sub-09: 7/14), confirming residual CVD under standard viewing conditions. A fitted $g > 2$ is therefore internally inconsistent with confirmed CVD status: the model claims overcompensation where the participants demonstrably show impairment. Therefore, the saturated boundary is a model failure, not a valid parameter estimate.

The structural cause is a degree-of-freedom deficit. The function $\delta\theta = (2 - g)\cdot\delta\theta_{\rm Mach}$ displaces hues only along the fixed confusion axis. No value of $g$ can account for distortions in directions orthogonal to that axis. R+C is therefore excluded as a filter candidate.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{A common cortical model fits both CVD cases}
\label{sec:results:twocomp}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The 2-component cortical model fit both participants using the same functional form. The deutan and protan cases differ in $(\hat\beta_s, \hat\beta_c)$, not in model structure. We fit the two-component parameters $(\beta_s, \beta_c)$ for each participant and inverted the fitted transform to a stimulus-space filter.

For sub-08 (deutan, $\theta_{\rm conf} = 150^\circ$), the selected loss combination $\gamma_{\rm OY} + L_{\rm RDM}^{(V2)}$ yielded $(\hat\beta_s, \hat\beta_c) = (6^\circ, -42^\circ)$. The held-out test-loss was $\overline{L}_{\rm test} = -2.36$ (IQR $= 2.15$), lower than the alternative candidate ($\overline{L}_{\rm test} = -1.14$; Supplementary~\S S2). The HC-resample parameter IQR was $(8^\circ, 2^\circ)$. The strict 7-fold LOO $\hat\beta_c$ range was $[-46^\circ, -38^\circ]$; all values are negative, confirming a stable sign across held-out reference sets.

For sub-09 (protan, $\theta_{\rm conf} = 16^\circ$), the selected loss combination $\gamma_{\rm all} + L_{\rm RDM}^{(V1)}$ yielded $(\hat\beta_s, \hat\beta_c) = (2^\circ, +24^\circ)$. The held-out test-loss was $\overline{L}_{\rm test} = -1.54$ (IQR $= 1.42$).
The HC-resample parameter IQR was $(0^\circ, 0^\circ)$, and $\langle 87.7\%\rangle$ of resamples recovered the same $45^\circ$ bin.
The strict 7-fold LOO IQR was $(0^\circ, 0^\circ)$. However, this stability is specific to the PCA-basis loss: the argmin shifts under the alternative SRM-basis reduction, so the recovered position is metric-dependent.

The RDM atom in each subject's winning combination was consistent with the ROI showing significant geometric disparity: V2 for sub-08 ($p = 0.040$) and V1 for sub-09 ($p = 0.007$, Section~\ref{sec:results:geometry}). The two criteria were independent: the loss combination was selected by held-out test-loss, and the significant disparity ROI was identified by Crawford--Howell tests on disparity amplitude.

On held-out folds, both fitted distortions reproduced the observed CVD geometry, beating the no-correction baseline on all 7 folds and ranking in the top $5$--$8\%$ of grid combinations.
Relative to the HC noise ceiling --- the best fit achievable given HC data noise, estimated from split-half HC amplitude reliability \cite{lagecastellanos2018} and conservative because CVD amplitude noise is excluded --- the fits reached $52\%$ (sub-08) and $67\%$ (sub-09) of the way from baseline to ceiling (held-out losses and ceiling values in Supplementary~\S S2).

\subsection{Neural data identifies what behavior cannot}
\label{sec:results:neural_role}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Fitting behavioral ($\gamma$) and neural (RDM) loss atoms independently reveals three benefits of utilizing the neural component.

First, the neural term captures a protan cortical direction that the behavioral loss cannot resolve. Sub-09's behavioral-only fit yielded $\hat\beta_c \approx +4^\circ$ and did not beat the no-correction baseline ($\Delta L = +0.01$, 4/7 folds). The RDM term captures a signal that the behavioral loss cannot detect for this participant.

Second, the neural term stabilizes the argmin for sub-08. The behavioral-only and combined fits share the same argmin $(6^\circ, -42^\circ)$, confirming that the behavioral atom independently locates the deutan direction; the neural-only fit independently corroborates this direction with a meaningful estimate ($\hat\beta_c = -26^\circ$; Supplementary~\S S2).
Adding the RDM atom reduced the boundary saturation rate from $23\%$ to $9.3\%$, sharpening the argmin without shifting it.

Third, the neural term enhances parameter stability by reducing its IQR in both participants. For sub-08, adding the neural term reduced the HC-resample IQR from $(18^\circ, 6^\circ)$ to $(8^\circ, 2^\circ)$ (PCA) and $(10^\circ, 4^\circ)$ (SRM). For sub-09, the IQR narrowed from $(6^\circ, 4^\circ)$ to $(0^\circ, 0^\circ)$ (PCA, deterministic) and $(0^\circ, 2^\circ)$ (SRM).
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Identifiability: mechanism class recoverable, per-axis magnitude not}
\label{sec:results:identifiability}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

These fits recover the distortion's mechanism class and the direction of its dominant axis, but not its precise per-axis magnitudes. Parameter recovery fails across all pre-specified identifiability checks (0/6 significant after FDR correction, BH $\alpha = 0.05$; Supplementary~\S S3). The voxel-level parameter-recovery test yielded $f_{10^\circ} < 0.30$ for both production candidates. The non-dominant axes ($|\hat\beta_s| \leq 6^\circ$ for both participants) fall below the effective parameter uncertainty of $\sim\!20^\circ$--$25^\circ$ and are not recoverable (Supplementary~\S S3). The dominant axis ($|\hat\beta_c| = 42^\circ$ for sub-08) exceeds this uncertainty and is partially recoverable: when CVD responses are simulated from a known distortion and re-fitted, the recovered $\hat\beta_c$ deviates from the true value by $4.7^\circ$ on average (Supplementary~\S S3).

Consistent with the partial recoverability of the dominant axis, the sign of $\hat\beta_c$ --- the distortion direction --- is stable across held-out reference sets (Section~\ref{sec:results:twocomp}). Sub-08 consistently returns $\hat\beta_c < 0$ across all LOO folds and resamples. Sub-09 consistently returns $\hat\beta_c > 0$ under the PCA-basis loss; sign stability under the SRM-basis loss was not verified (Supplementary~\S S3). The distortion direction is recoverable even when its magnitude is not.

The loss landscape shows where this magnitude indeterminacy lies. Although each participant's selected fit is the global minimum on the full data (Figure~\ref{fig:landscape}), the per-resample argmins do not all concentrate there.
For sub-09, $87.7\%$ recur at the selected fit, but a secondary basin near $(32^\circ, 0^\circ)$ marks a metric-dependent alternative.
For sub-08, they disperse along the $\beta_c$ axis, leaving its magnitude loosely constrained.

\subsection{Per-subject stimulus-space filter}
\label{sec:results:filter}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The per-subject filter $\delta\theta^{\rm filt}_k = \tilde\theta_k - \theta_k$ is the pre-image of the fitted 2-component transform (Section~\ref{sec:methods:filter}).

For sub-08 (deutan, $\hat\beta_s = 6^\circ$, $\hat\beta_c = -42^\circ$, $\theta_{\rm conf} = 150^\circ$), the dominant $\hat\beta_c$ term produces a large-amplitude correction that varies asymmetrically across hues (mean $|\delta\theta| = 26.3^\circ$; Figure~\ref{fig:filter}).

For sub-09 (protan, $\hat\beta_s = 2^\circ$, $\hat\beta_c = +24^\circ$, $\theta_{\rm conf} = 16^\circ$), the smaller $\hat\beta_c$ produces a more targeted correction concentrated near the protan confusion axis (mean $|\delta\theta| = 16.2^\circ$; Figure~\ref{fig:filter}).

The two fits differ in the magnitude and direction of the dominant confusion-axis term ($\hat\beta_c = -42^\circ$ for sub-08 vs.\ $+24^\circ$ for sub-09).

\subsection{Behavioral and neural filter evaluation (sub-08)}
\label{sec:results:filter_eval}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

We evaluated the personalized filter in a second session in the deutan participant (sub-08; sub-09 was not collected). On the primary neural index --- LOCO forward-tuning correlation to the canonical hue map --- the personalized filter was more healthy-control-like than the deployed macOS accessibility filter in all four ROIs (Fig.~\ref{fig:filter_eval}A; personalized $\rho$: V1 $+0.21$, V2 $+0.10$, V3 $+0.05$, V4 $+0.18$; deployed $\rho$: V1 $-0.32$, V2 $-0.19$, V3 $-0.32$, V4 $-0.39$). The contrast was robust: the personalized condition remained more HC-like after dropping any single run (leave-one-run separated in every ROI) and after re-extracting both conditions on the baseline voxel set (personalized$-$deployed $\Delta\rho = 0.37$--$0.57$). The deployed filter drove forward-tuning strongly negative, distorting rather than restoring the canonical hue map. The effect holds across ROIs rather than at V1 alone: at the baseline voxel set the personalized filter sat at the HC level at V2 ($d = +0.04$) and within one HC standard deviation at V3 and V4, while V1's larger native effect ($d = +0.97$) attenuated under voxel matching ($d = -0.82$), indicating it was partly driven by occipital coverage.

The filters were not separable on every readout. LORO classification accuracy was near-equal across the deployed, personalized, and HC conditions ($\approx 0.69$--$0.72$ at V1), so both filters preserved color decodability while only the personalized filter restored the forward-tuning geometry. SRM disparity favored the personalized over the deployed filter in all ROIs, but the no-filter baseline had the lowest disparity of the three (surviving run-count matching), and the shared-space RDM agreed: neither filter was more HC-like than no filter by representational geometry (Fig.~\ref{fig:filter_eval}C,D). The personalized filter thus restores forward-tuning to HC levels but does not normalize the color geometry. That geometry remains unlike HC's and more compressed.

Behaviorally, both filters worked: the baseline deutan deficit (JND mean $|z| = 2.24$; the orange--yellow, yellow--green, and yellow--purple pairs all exceeding the HC empirical maximum) was eliminated by both the deployed filter ($|z| = 0.85$) and the personalized filter ($|z| = 0.78$), and RSVP identification accuracy rose from $0.81$ at baseline to $0.97$ under both, abolishing the yellow--green and purple--magenta confusions. The two filters could not be ranked behaviorally (Wilcoxon $p = 0.84$) and failed on different pairs (the personalized filter over-corrected orange--yellow below the HC range); the single-subject, single-run, cross-session design with a shared-pipeline confound precludes a behavioral ranking. Both a well-configured deployed filter and the personalized filter thus restore sub-08's discrimination to near-control levels, and the personalized filter's distinctive advantage is confined to the pipeline-robust neural forward-tuning contrast.
